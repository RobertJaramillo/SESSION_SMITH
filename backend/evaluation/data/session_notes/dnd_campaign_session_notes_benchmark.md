# The Ledger Road Campaign

## Session 0

### World Overview
The campaign takes place in Veyr, a damp river kingdom built over older imperial roads, buried toll shrines, and frontier forts that nobody has had the money to maintain properly in two generations. Trade is controlled by guild ledgers and river charters, but the old border country is starting to behave strangely again.

### Campaign Objective
The party believes they have been hired to escort a disputed merchant ledger from the river town of Brackenford to the provincial capital of Ilyr’s Gate. They are told the job is routine, except that three previous couriers have gone missing on the western road.

## Session 1

Narrative Summary

First real session after character intros. Party met in Brackenford at The Three Eels, where Master Pell Ardwine hired them to carry the sealed ledger case to Ilyr’s Gate. The group: Mara Vale the ex-soldier fighter, Tovin Reed the human wizard who keeps overexplaining everything, Sister Elian the cleric of the River Saint, Kestrel the half-elf rogue, and Bran Calder the ranger from the western reeds. We spent longer than expected on the tavern scene because the players immediately distrusted Pell, the innkeeper, the stable boy, and each other.

They examined the ledger case and found three wax seals: Ardwine Mercantile, the Brackenford council stamp, and an older imperial sunburst mark none of them recognized except Tovin, who rolled well enough to know it was not current royal usage. Kestrel wanted to open it immediately. Elian argued that breaking a lawful seal before accepting the job was theft, which turned into the first moral argument of the campaign. They accepted after negotiating hazard pay up front and a promise of additional coin on delivery.

On the road west, they found signs of an abandoned camp belonging to one of the missing couriers: cold ashes, one torn boot, and two horseshoe prints that stopped abruptly in mud. Bran got very invested in the tracks. The party was ambushed near dusk by three mud-smeared bandits and one trained mastiff. The fight was easy but messy. Mara tried to disarm the leader and accidentally knocked him into the ditch, where Kestrel threatened him with a cooking knife instead of her actual dagger. They took one bandit alive: Jory Pike, who said they were paid to scare couriers off, not kill anyone.

They camped off-road after arguing about whether Jory should be tied to a tree or given soup. Elian gave him soup. Session ended with Tovin noticing the ledger case was faintly warm near the old imperial seal.

## Session 2

Narrative Summary

Started with morning interrogation of Jory. He gave them the name “Blue Aster,” which he thought was a person but is probably going to become a sign/symbol now because everyone latched onto it hard. He said payment came through a dead drop at the old milestone south of Harrow Bend. Bran wanted to go there immediately, even though it was out of the way. The others debated whether this was a trap for almost half an hour. They eventually decided that because it was obviously a trap, they should spring it in daylight.

At the milestone they found a hollow under the cracked imperial marker containing two clipped silver coins, blue thread, and a scrap of vellum with the number 17 written in bookkeeping shorthand. Tovin used Detect Magic and got a faint abjuration trace from the marker itself, not the contents. Elian recognized the milestone as one of the saint-road markers built over older pilgrim stones. Good religion roll, so I gave her the detail that River Saint chapels often reuse older sites because the river church likes continuity more than accuracy.

Instead of continuing to Ilyr’s Gate, the party detoured to Harrow Bend, a small ferry hamlet. This skipped the roadside shrine encounter I prepped. They met Corven Mott, the ferryman, who became suspiciously friendly because Kestrel tipped him too much. Corven said two riders crossed three nights ago with a covered cart and no horse visible pulling it. This freaked out Bran. The party searched the ferry landing and found black silt caked under the boards, smelling like old coins and river mud.

There was a short fight with four “drowned rats” in Corven’s grain shed after the wizard poked a barrel. Not dangerous, but the rats had bits of blue thread in their teeth. Players immediately decided the rats were spies. Session ended with the group agreeing to spend the night in Harrow Bend so Mara could repair armor and Kestrel could follow Corven, despite Corven not currently being guilty of anything.

## Session 3

Narrative Summary

Whole session stayed in Harrow Bend, which I did not expect. Kestrel followed Corven and learned he is mostly guilty of watering ale and not reporting ferry taxes. The group had breakfast with Old Mother Wren after Elian helped carry firewood. Wren told them the old western road used to run straight to Blackstone Fort before the crown rerouted traffic south after the “Sable Year.” Nobody knew what Sable Year meant except Tovin, who failed history by one. I let him know only that it sounded like a bureaucratic name for a disaster.

The party visited the ruined toll office by the ferry. Inside, they found a wall niche where a ledger shelf had been removed recently. Mara noticed drag marks and boot prints matching a heavy person with a limp. Bran tracked those prints toward the reed beds. Instead of charging in, the party spent a long time making a plan involving bells, rope, and Corven’s spare punt. It partly worked. They found a hidden reed hut being used by a woman named Salla Vint, who claimed to be a tax investigator for the capital. She had forged papers, but the forgery was good enough that the party was divided.

Salla tried to flee by drinking a potion and turning invisible. Kestrel anticipated this somehow and threw flour from Corven’s kitchen, which was great. They captured her after a very stupid chase through knee-deep mud. She admitted she had been paid to recover “page seventeen” from old toll ledgers, not the party’s ledger case. She did not know who paid her, only that messages came with blue aster petals pressed into wax.

Session ended when they opened the removed toll shelf compartment and found a half-rotted toll ledger from 138 years ago. Page 17 had been cut out. The last readable entry before the missing page mentioned “military traffic sealed under Crown silence.”

## Session 4

Narrative Summary

Travel session from Harrow Bend toward Ilyr’s Gate, finally. Party decided to bring Salla along bound but not gagged, because Elian wanted her to have “a chance to confess properly.” This was useful because Salla explained that clipped silver is used by smugglers to mark partial payment without naming a patron. She also warned that if the ledger case really has an imperial seal, opening it carelessly could trigger an old contract ward. Tovin immediately wanted to open it more.

They stopped at Saint Orlan’s roadside shrine, the encounter they skipped last week but moved north. Shrine was abandoned except for a traveling tinker, Dobb Renn, who had taken shelter there. Players loved Dobb immediately because I gave him a squeaky handcart wheel. He sold them lamp oil, chalk, and a dented tea kettle. He also had a blue aster pressed in his prayer book, but claimed it came from his daughter’s grave near Ilyr’s Gate. This made everyone feel bad for suspecting him. Kestrel still stole the flower, then felt bad and paid double for chalk.

At night the shrine bell rang by itself. Four roadwardens in old rusted mail appeared out of the fog, not undead exactly, more like oath echoes. They demanded “manifest, seal, and road right.” The party showed the ledger case. The echoes bowed to the imperial seal and asked whether “Fort Blackstone has been relieved.” Mara lied and said yes. The echoes let them pass but one touched her sword and left frost on the blade.

They discussed turning back to Blackstone immediately, but Salla reminded them they were being paid to reach Ilyr’s Gate. Session ended with the party seeing the lights of the capital road and noticing that the ledger case had cooled after the roadwardens accepted it.

## Session 5

Narrative Summary

Arrived at Ilyr’s Gate. I described it as a crowded river capital with two bridges, too many cranes, and old imperial stones built into newer walls. The players mostly cared about where to stable the horses and whether Salla could be turned in for money. They went to Ardwine Mercantile’s counting house and met Lysa Ardwine, Pell’s older sister and actual boss. Lysa was composed, annoyed that they were late, and very interested in the intact seals. She paid the balance but refused to explain the imperial mark.

Kestrel tried to palm the ledger case during the handoff and failed. Not enough to get caught by guards, but Lysa definitely noticed. Instead of opening the case publicly, Lysa took them into a private archive room and revealed she hired them because couriers with normal company guards kept disappearing. The ledger inside, once opened with her key and Elian’s blessing to suppress the ward, listed purchases of stone, grain, lamp oil, and coffin nails routed to Blackstone Fort for twelve years after official records say it was abandoned. Page 17 was again missing.

This was the first big “oh” moment. The party shifted from suspicious of the employer to suspicious of the provincial government. Lysa offered a second job: find who has been editing historic trade records and why the old fort is appearing in active accounts. They accepted after negotiating access to the Ardwine archive instead of more money, which surprised me.

They turned Salla over not to city guards but to Lysa’s house security after Salla begged not to be taken to the magistrates. Tovin spent the evening researching Sable Year and got one useful detail: every public account says a plague emptied the western forts, but casualty ledgers are sealed by military court order.

## Session 6

Narrative Summary

City research and errands session. Not much combat, but a lot of useful character stuff. Mara visited a swordsmith about the frost mark on her blade. The smith, Harl Brunn, said the mark looked like “oath frost” and refused to grind it off because old road magic can punish disrespect. This made Mara nervous; she has been treating the roadwarden lie like a joke. Elian went to the River Saint cathedral and learned that Saint Orlan’s shrine predates the church by at least 400 years, which annoyed the deacon she asked.

Kestrel and Bran checked markets for blue aster. They found it is not a local flower; it grows in the dry uplands west of Blackstone, not near rivers. This made Dobb’s story about his daughter’s grave slightly odd unless someone imported the flower. Kestrel admitted stealing it from the prayer book, and Elian gave her a look. The party then spent too long shopping. Tovin bought sealing wax and three notebooks. Bran bought a better cloak and named it “the serious cloak,” which somehow became a table bit.

Archive time introduced Meren Halst, an elderly clerk who knows the Ardwine records better than Lysa does. Meren confirmed page 17 is missing from six different ledgers spanning 90 years. He also said the handwriting on later Blackstone supply entries matches no known clerk and appears in books that should have been stored in different rooms. Tovin became obsessed with the idea of a ghost accountant.

Only encounter was a tailing scene. Two dock toughs followed them after they left the archive. Party confronted them in an alley and negotiated instead of fighting. The toughs said they were paid by a woman with a veil and a scent of crushed flowers to learn whether the party had opened the case. They fled after Mara cracked one’s cudgel.

## Session 7

Narrative Summary

The players decided they needed official permission to inspect military records, which I did not expect because they usually avoid bureaucracy. They went to the Hall of Petitions and spent nearly the whole first half dealing with clerks. This introduced Magistrate Iven Tor, who absolutely stonewalled them until Elian name-dropped the River Saint cathedral and Mara showed her old campaign token. Tor got nervous at the word Blackstone and insisted the plague records were sealed for public safety, which obviously convinced the party there was no plague.

Kestrel slipped away and searched the clerk bins. She found a rejected petition from Dobb Renn requesting permission to exhume his daughter, Mara Renn, whose grave had supposedly been moved during bridge expansion. This hit the party harder than expected because Mara the PC shares the name. They immediately wanted to find Dobb again. The petition included a cemetery plot number that had been corrected twice in different ink.

At the cemetery, they discovered the grave existed but the stone was newer than the burial date. Bran noticed the soil had settled wrong. Elian performed a quiet rite and sensed no body beneath the grave, only a small iron road token buried where the heart should have been. When Tovin touched it, he heard wagon wheels and someone whispering numbers: “seventeen, seventeen, seventeen.”

The veiled woman finally appeared at the cemetery gate with two hired duelists. She offered to buy the road token and all their notes for 500 gp. Party refused, but surprisingly politely. Combat broke out after Bran shot first. Duelists were competent; Kestrel went down briefly from a crit, first scary moment. The veiled woman escaped using a smoke bead and left blue aster petals burning on the ground.

## Session 8

Narrative Summary

Recovery and planning session after cemetery fight. Kestrel woke up angry and embarrassed, then insisted they find better armor before chasing anyone. Shopping took most of the first hour. They bought healing supplies, caltrops, a crowbar, and a very ugly mule named Button because Bran said the western road would ruin city horses. Button immediately became beloved. I had not planned a mule NPC.

They found Dobb at a cheap lodging house near the east bridge. He was devastated but not surprised to learn the grave was empty. He admitted his daughter Mara vanished five years ago after taking work copying old toll records for the magistrates. Officials told him she died of fever and gave him a sealed coffin. He never saw the body. Dobb produced one letter from Mara mentioning “the road beneath the road” and “the fort that still eats.” The players got quiet at that one.

Lysa Ardwine arranged a private meeting with Meren and revealed Ardwine had also lost clerks over the years, but the family hid it to avoid panic and liability. This led to a heated roleplay scene where Elian accused Lysa of valuing ledgers over lives. Lysa did not deny it, which oddly made the players respect her more. She offered supplies if the party would go west to Blackstone and find proof before Magistrate Tor buries everything again.

They spent the second half preparing for travel. Tovin copied old maps; Bran compared them to current routes and noticed the old imperial road should cross a ravine that does not exist on modern maps. Party decided to leave at dawn with Dobb coming along despite everyone telling him not to. Session ended with Kestrel giving Dobb back the stolen blue aster from his prayer book.

## Session 9

Narrative Summary

Left Ilyr’s Gate and traveled west on the less-used military road. This was mostly travel, camp logistics, and character conversations. Mara and Dobb had an awkward exchange about his daughter sharing her name. He apologized for staring at her during watch. Elian tried to comfort him and ended up getting him to talk about Mara Renn as a real person: stubborn, good with numbers, hated onions, wanted to see the sea. Nice table moment, not plot-heavy but useful.

First travel complication was a washed-out bridge. The party spent a long time engineering a crossing for Button. Tovin used Tenser’s Floating Disk for packs, Bran rigged lines, and Kestrel crawled across first to secure rope. No fight, but very tense because everyone cared more about the mule than themselves. They succeeded but lost one crate of lamp oil when a rope slipped. I ruled two flasks survived.

At the old watch post beyond the bridge, they found fresh camp signs: expensive boot prints, pipe ash, and a broken crossbow bolt with blue lacquer. Bran tracked scouts moving parallel to the road. The party chose not to pursue, deciding the scouts wanted to lure them away. Correct call. At night, the road token from Mara Renn’s grave vibrated when placed near an imperial milestone. Tovin used it like a compass and found a buried stone plate under the milestone.

They pried it up and saw stairs descending into darkness, but only for a few seconds before the opening sealed itself again. The plate showed the sunburst seal and the number XVII. Everyone shouted at once. Session ended with the party marking the spot and deciding whether to dig, camp, or rush to Blackstone.

## Session 10

Narrative Summary

Party chose to camp and study the sealed stair instead of rushing to Blackstone. They set watches, hid Button behind a scrub wall, and used chalk to mark the stone plate. During second watch, Kestrel heard scraping from below the road, like someone dragging chains under the dirt. She woke everyone quietly. Tovin placed the iron token on the plate and Elian recited part of the River Saint road blessing from memory. This opened the stair properly, though the milestone cracked from top to bottom.

Below was a narrow imperial waystation, not a full dungeon: storage alcoves, old toll bars, and a dry cistern. It smelled like dust and pennies. The party found skeletal remains of three couriers in a side alcove, including one wearing the torn boot from Session 1. They had been killed by crossbow bolts and dragged below, not vanished magically. This grounded the mystery nicely. Mara got angry and practical, searching bolt wounds and angles while everyone else got grim.

Combat with two road-bound constructs made of mail, wood, and milestone fragments. They were not too hard but resisted nonmagical weapons until Elian used sacred flame and Mara’s frost-marked sword did full damage. Mara is now worried the oath frost is useful, which is exactly the wrong lesson. Kestrel used caltrops even though constructs probably should not care; I let it slow one because the table loved the visual.

They found a locked iron dispatch tube in the cistern. Kestrel opened it after three attempts and one snapped pick. Inside was page 17 from a Blackstone quartermaster ledger. It listed “civilian copyists retained under emergency authority” and names including Mara Renn. It also listed ongoing ration allotments for “sealed personnel below road grade.” Session ended with the party realizing Dobb’s daughter may have been taken alive into whatever lies under Blackstone.

## Session 11

Narrative Summary

The party pushed hard toward Blackstone instead of resting properly, which I warned them would mean exhaustion checks if they kept going after dark. They accepted that because Dobb was practically vibrating after seeing Mara Renn’s name on the ledger page. Travel felt tense. Bran spotted the blue-lacquer scouts twice, always just beyond bow range, and the party finally decided to set a false camp and circle back. This worked better than expected. Kestrel and Bran caught one scout, a young woman named Vey Lark, while the other escaped west.

Vey was not a fanatic. She said she worked for “the Aster Table,” which she described as a private society of families who inherited obligations from imperial road contracts. She believed the party was about to break a containment pact at Blackstone. Elian got very serious about the word containment. Vey insisted the fort was not abandoned but “closed,” and that every few decades somebody gets greedy, romantic, or stupid and tries to reopen it. Tovin asked which one they were. Vey said probably all three.

They reached the outer hills around Blackstone by late afternoon. The fort itself was visible across a scrub valley: broken curtain wall, one leaning tower, and a dry moat full of thorn trees. Smoke rose from inside, which ruined the party’s assumption that it was empty. They debated sending Kestrel alone, then remembered the cemetery fight and decided against splitting. Good growth.

Approach encounter was with thorn goblins living in the moat, but the party negotiated. The goblins called themselves the Bristle Choir and demanded shiny tolls to cross “their ditch.” Tovin gave them clipped silver; this accidentally impressed them. The goblin speaker, Yip-of-the-Root, warned that “the stone mouths sing at night” and that humans in blue coats enter the fort but do not always leave.

Session ended with the party hidden in a ruined goat shed outside the fort, watching blue-coated guards change watch at a postern gate that should have been collapsed.
