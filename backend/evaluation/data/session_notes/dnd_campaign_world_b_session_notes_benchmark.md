# Ashes of the Moon Gate

## Session 0

### World Overview
The world is Caldrin, a coast-and-mountain realm still rebuilding after a lunar fragment fell into the northern sea eighty years ago and changed tides, magic, and old burial customs. Most people worry more about taxes, storms, and missing caravans than cosmic history.

### Campaign Objective
The party believes they have been hired to escort a surveyor to reopen a damaged mountain pass before winter trade fails. They are told the only complication is that the previous survey crew stopped sending reports from the village of Moonstairs.

## Session 1

Narrative Summary

First session introduced the party at Grey Gull Yard in the port city of Valmire: Dain the dwarf fighter and retired harbor guard, Sera the human diviner, Lio the halfling rogue and chronic gambler, Amathe the elven cleric of the Hearth Bell, and Corren the half-orc druid from the pine uplands. They met Surveyor Kel Vos, who was anxious, underfunded, and very specific about not calling this an expedition. The contract was to escort him inland to Moonstairs and verify whether the old pass road could be repaired.

The party immediately distrusted the contract because the pay was too good. Lio tried to gamble the advance into more advance and lost 6 gp to a dockhand named Pearce. Sera checked the sealed map case and noticed the wax held a crescent-and-gate mark rather than the normal city survey seal. Kel claimed that was an old mountain office stamp. Nobody believed him, but they left anyway because winter contracts were drying up.

On the first road day they found a shrine bell hanging from a burned tree. Amathe rang it, and everyone heard a second bell answer from underground. Bandits attacked soon after, but they were hungry deserters, not professionals. Dain talked two down while Corren tangled the others in roots. One deserter said Moonstairs pays silver for “moon glass” and that the old survey crew found something under the pass.

They camped with one captured deserter, Noll, who promised he was done robbing people if they would let him keep his boots. Session ended with the map case clicking once by itself at midnight.

## Session 2

Narrative Summary

Morning started with Noll bargaining for freedom. He knew a charcoal track that would cut half a day off the road to Moonstairs. The party argued whether to trust him, then decided the shortcut was worth it because Kel kept checking the sky like weather mattered more than it should. The charcoal track was muddy, narrow, and full of abandoned kiln pits. Corren enjoyed identifying old burn marks while everyone else complained about soot.

They found a ruined charcoal camp with three fresh graves and one open pit full of pale glassy stones. Sera determined the stones were not naturally cooled slag. They gave off faint divination magic and reflected the moon even in daylight. Lio pocketed two before anyone discussed safety. Amathe noticed the graves had Hearth Bell burial cords tied incorrectly, left-handed instead of right-handed. That became a clue because local priests would not make that mistake.

Encounter was with a wounded black bear whose fur had moon glass shards embedded in it. Corren calmed it enough that they did not have to kill it, though Dain still got clawed badly when Lio moved too close. They removed some shards and let it limp away. Kel wanted to collect the shards for survey evidence; Corren said absolutely not.

They reached Moonstairs at dusk. The village is built on switchback terraces below a cliff road, with too many shuttered houses and blue-white lanterns burning in windows even before dark. The reeve, Tamsin Grell, welcomed them politely but told them the pass is closed by village vote. Session ended with Sera seeing one of the missing survey crew alive in an upstairs window, then gone.

## Session 3

Narrative Summary

Moonstairs investigation session. The party stayed at The Lantern Goat, run by Mira Fen, who warned them not to whistle after moonrise. Naturally Lio whistled within ten minutes. Nothing happened immediately, which made it worse. The village had a tense, practical feel: people repairing roofs, salting meat, avoiding talk about the upper pass. Kel tried to visit the old survey office but Tamsin assigned a “guide,” really a watcher named Brant.

The party split carefully. Dain and Kel checked the locked survey shed. They found broken measuring rods, blood on a wall map, and a ledger page listing pass-stones numbered 1 through 19. Page 13 was torn out. Sera and Amathe interviewed Mira, who admitted the missing crew came back after three nights but “not all at once.” One had no shadow. Corren went to look at village goats and found several staring uphill in unison.

At moonrise, Lio’s earlier whistle was answered from the cistern behind the inn. The group opened it and found a wet rope ladder leading down, not village construction. They descended into a small undercellar where moon glass had grown through old masonry. There they met Eddic Hale, one of the missing surveyors, alive but exhausted. He begged them not to reopen the pass because “the gate counts walkers both ways.”

Before they could ask more, Brant and two villagers arrived armed, demanding Eddic be turned over to the reeve. No fight yet. Session ended with Dain stepping between them and saying nobody gets dragged anywhere tonight.

## Session 4

Narrative Summary

Opened with cellar standoff. Brant insisted Eddic was sick and belonged in village care. Amathe asked what care means. He did not answer well. Dain de-escalated by offering to bring Eddic to the inn common room instead of letting either side hide him. This turned into a late-night village meeting with Tamsin, Mira, Kel, Eddic, and half the town pretending not to listen from doorways.

Eddic explained in pieces. The survey crew reached the old Moon Gate at the pass summit, an imperial arch supposedly decorative. During moonrise, it opened onto a road of white dust and black sky. Three crew crossed by accident chasing a dropped lantern. Four came back, including one who was not part of the crew. That extra person looked like their dead cook, Harlon, and had no shadow. Tamsin admitted the village has been hiding the returned crew because outsiders might burn Moonstairs or force the pass open anyway.

The party investigated the shadowless cook, Harlon, in a locked storehouse. He was polite, hungry, and very wrong. He remembered dying in a rockslide eight years ago but also remembered cooking for the survey crew last week. Sera used a mirror and saw not his reflection but a dark stair descending behind him. Combat happened when Harlon’s mouth filled with white dust and he tried to walk through the wall toward the pass. They restrained him without killing him.

Session ended with the village agreeing to let the party inspect the pass at dawn, but only if Tamsin, Brant, and Kel come too.

## Session 5

Narrative Summary

Dawn climb to the pass. This was travel/exploration and first major location reveal. Moonstairs earns its name: old switchbacks carved like huge steps into the cliff, with ruined rest platforms every few hundred feet. Corren noticed pines stop growing above the ninth platform. Lio kept trying to spot secret caches and nearly stepped off a scree edge. Dain tied him to a rope, which Lio hated.

At pass-stone 7 they found abandoned survey gear and a boot frozen into glassy rime despite mild weather. At pass-stone 10, Tamsin showed them the rockslide scar where Harlon died years ago. The stones were numbered in old imperial script, and Sera realized every odd stone had a small crescent notch pointing uphill. Page 13 from the ledger would probably describe the thirteenth stone, where the gate stands.

They reached Moon Gate near noon. It was a broken arch of pale basalt and moon glass, decorative at first glance but set directly into the road. Kel finally admitted he was hired by a private patron, not the city survey office, to prove the gate still opened. The party nearly threw him down the mountain. Before they could decide, the arch cast a shadow the wrong direction even though the moon was not visible.

Encounter was with glass mites crawling from cracks, attracted to Lio’s pocketed stones. This exposed his theft. Fight was easy but annoying, with mites chewing straps and map edges. Afterward, Amathe made Lio put the stones in a lead cooking pot. Session ended when Sera read worn writing on the arch: “One road, two skies, all crossings counted.”

## Session 6

Narrative Summary

They chose to stay at the gate until moonrise, despite Tamsin arguing they should retreat and return with a plan. Good adventurer nonsense. They spent the afternoon making preparations: chalk lines, rope anchors, bell cords from Amathe, and Corren arranging stones in what he called “a polite warning to nature.” Kel took notes while everyone glared at him. Lio apologized for the moon glass but not very convincingly.

At moonrise, the arch opened silently. Through it they saw a road of white dust under a black sky, with their own mountain pass continuing on the other side but ruined and moonlit. The ropes tied to the arch went slack like they were much longer than measured. A voice counted each visible creature on the pass, including Button-sized mountain goats far away. The count ended one too high.

Nobody crossed fully, but Harlon, still restrained down in the village, somehow appeared on the far side of the gate and waved. This scared them more than a monster. Sera tried to communicate with a minor illusion number sequence. The gate answered by showing flashes of the missing survey crew walking in circles around a distant bell tower.

Combat was with moon-warped goats that charged down the slope after being counted. It was silly and dangerous because of cliffs. Dain got shoved prone, Corren used animal handling and failed, and Lio saved Kel from falling despite hating him. The gate closed at moonset, leaving behind a strip of white dust and one brass bell clapper.

## Session 7

Narrative Summary

Return to Moonstairs with evidence. They expected panic, but the village was mostly irritated because goats had broken fences during the moonrise event. This made the supernatural feel grounded. Tamsin organized repairs while the party tried to explain the gate. Mira Fen produced an old village song about “the bell that calls the counted home,” which nobody had taken seriously because it is usually sung to children who wander.

The brass clapper fit no village bell, but Amathe found a matching mounting scar in the abandoned upper chapel. The chapel had been locked for years after its bell cracked. Inside, the party found old names carved into pew backs, including Harlon’s name twice: once from eight years ago and once fresh. Sera detected the chapel as a weak anchor to the same magic as the gate.

This became a research/downtime session. Dain helped repair goat pens and gained villager trust. Lio played dice with teenagers and learned that some children dream of walking on white roads. Corren checked the bear from Session 2 and found it sleeping near the charcoal track, less glassy now. Kel wrote a confession letter naming his patron: Lady Vossryn Thale, a Valmire antiquities collector and city council donor.

At night they tested the cracked chapel bell with the brass clapper. It rang once without sound, and Harlon in the storehouse cast a shadow for about ten seconds. Session ended with the party deciding the bell might retrieve or stabilize counted people if repaired.

## Session 8

Narrative Summary

The party decided repairing the chapel bell required a bellfounder, not random adventurer hammering, which is annoyingly responsible. Moonstairs had no bellfounder, but Mira knew one in Low Cairn, a mining village two days north. Rather than return to Valmire, they chose the mining road. Kel stayed in Moonstairs under Tamsin’s supervision to help repair damage and because the party did not trust him with the gate.

Travel north was mostly weather and logistics. Early snow in the high pines slowed them. Corren foraged well, but Lio lost a glove and complained constantly. They found a pilgrim cairn with small bells tied to sticks; several bells rang as they passed even with no wind. Amathe left a proper Hearth Bell cord there. This mattered later because the ringing stopped after she corrected the knot.

Encounter was with three miners fleeing Low Cairn. They said the mine bell had begun ringing underground and men who answered it came back with silver dust in their eyes. One miner, Daska, begged the party not to go there and then asked if they had food in the same breath. The party fed them and learned Lady Vossryn’s agents had bought old moon ore from Low Cairn for years.

They reached the village at dusk. Low Cairn’s surface streets were quiet, but hammering echoed from the closed mine. The bellfounder, Orro Vane, was missing below with his apprentice. Session ended at the mine gate with Dain very happy to finally be in a normal dangerous hole.

## Session 9

Narrative Summary

Mine descent. Low Cairn mine was a good contrast to Moonstairs: tight tunnels, practical workers’ marks, dripping water, and one deep shaft where a bell rang from below at irregular intervals. The party met Forewoman Pell, who was exhausted and angry that outsiders had finally shown up after years of buyers ignoring safety. She agreed to guide them to the sealed lower gallery if they promised to bring Orro back if alive.

The lower gallery had veins of moon ore, not glass, more like silver-black stone that hummed near the brass clapper. Sera realized the clapper was vibrating in rhythm with the mine bell. Lio, wearing one glove, kept touching ore until Amathe smacked his hand. They found miners standing asleep with eyes open, silver dust in lashes. Corren used lesser restoration on one and woke him briefly; he said Orro was “casting a bell for the road under the moon.”

Combat came from ore husks: miners’ shadows pulled loose and hardened into pick-wielding silhouettes. Not deadly but creepy. Dain tanked in a tunnel while Lio dragged sleeping miners clear. Forewoman Pell fought with a pry bar and earned immediate respect. They found Orro and apprentice Simm in a forge cavern, alive and working compulsively on a small handbell made of moon ore.

Session ended with Orro saying the handbell must be completed before the next moonrise or “all counted sleepers stay asleep.”

## Session 10

Narrative Summary

Handbell decision session. Orro insisted the moon ore handbell was needed to wake counted sleepers, but Sera detected compulsion in the rhythm of his hammering. The party decided not to smash the bell immediately. Instead, they tried to finish it correctly by adding Hearth Bell rites and Corren’s pine ash to the casting. This was a nice compromise between arcane, divine, and natural approaches.

Skill challenge around the forge: Dain kept ore husks away, Amathe corrected the bell inscription, Sera guided timing from clapper vibrations, Corren cooled the mold with summoned water and pine ash, and Lio climbed the bell frame to cut a silver cord without knowing what it did. Cutting the cord freed Simm from compulsion but caused the mine bell below to ring continuously.

The finished handbell did wake the silver-eyed miners, but it also opened a small white road reflection in the forge wall. Through it they saw the distant bell tower again, closer this time, with missing surveyors and miners walking around it. One figure turned and had Lio’s missing glove tucked in a belt, which made no sense and unsettled everyone.

They escaped the mine as the lower gallery partially collapsed. Orro survived but lost hearing in one ear. Forewoman Pell said Vossryn’s agents would return for ore shipments in three days. Session ended with the party leveling to 3 and deciding whether to ambush the agents or return to Moonstairs with Orro.

## Session 11

Narrative Summary

Party chose to wait for Vossryn’s agents rather than rush back to Moonstairs. This surprised Orro, who really wanted away from the mine, but Forewoman Pell supported the ambush. They staged ore crates with harmless stone and hid in the loading shed. Lio insisted on being inside a crate and then complained about splinters. Sera predicted three agents; four came, so she is now annoyed at fate.

The agents were not cultists, just hard professionals led by Maerik Sol, a polite man with immaculate boots. He carried city contracts showing Low Cairn had legally sold “lunar mineral samples” for six years. Pell said nobody explained the samples were making miners sleepwalk. Maerik replied that mining always has risks, which ended any chance of friendly conversation.

Combat was quick but tactical. Two agents used smoke beads, one used a repeating hand crossbow, and Maerik tried to burn the paperwork rather than fight to the death. Dain knocked him out with the flat of his axe. Lio saved half-burned invoices and also stole Maerik’s boots because “immaculate villain boots are loot.” Amathe disapproved but did not make him return them.

They learned shipments went to Vossryn House in Valmire, but a private cache had been diverted to an abandoned observatory called Starbreak Lodge. Orro said the observatory was built before the lunar fall and overlooks the same alignment as Moon Gate. Session ended with the party finally returning toward Moonstairs, with prisoner Maerik, Orro, Simm, and too much evidence.

## Session 12

Return road through snow. This was intended as simple travel, but Maerik made it interesting by needling everyone. He argued Vossryn funds orphanages, weather towers, and half the city’s public lamps, so exposing her would hurt ordinary people. Amathe countered that ordinary people are already being hurt in mines. Dain threatened to gag him, then did after Maerik insulted Forewoman Pell’s bookkeeping.

At the pilgrim cairn from Session 8, the bells were gone. In their place were little strips of white cloth tied in wrong knots. Amathe fixed one and heard a child crying from the woods. The party followed and found two Moonstairs children sleepwalking uphill in the snow. They had blue-white lanterns and no memory of leaving home. Corren woke one with the handbell, but the other kept walking until Lio tackled him. The handbell works, but not perfectly.

Dain’s double had a scar he does not have yet. Lio’s double wore Maerik’s boots, which he currently has. Nobody attacked. The doubles passed through them like cold fog.

They reached Moonstairs near dawn to find the chapel bell ringing silently, even though no clapper had been installed. Session ended with Tamsin furious that children were taken while the party was away.

## Session 13

Narrative Summary

Chapel repair session. Orro inspected the cracked bell and said normal repair would take a week, but the moon ore handbell could serve as a tuning heart if mounted inside the crack. This would stabilize the bell but also connect it more strongly to the Moon Gate. The players hated both options. They spent a long time arguing in the chapel while villagers waited outside with tools and blankets.

Maerik offered Vossryn technical notes in exchange for better treatment. The notes showed that Starbreak Lodge has a model of the Moon Gate and a “counting lens” made from polished moon glass. Sera realized the lens might be amplifying the gate remotely. This gave the party a reason to fix the bell enough to protect the village, then go to the lodge.

Repair challenge involved Orro, Amathe, and Sera leading while everyone else handled interruptions. Harlon became lucid briefly and helped by naming the old bell founders carved in the chapel floor. This is odd because he should not know them. Lio noticed but did not interrupt because it was useful. Outside, Brant caught a villager trying to steal moon glass chips from the repair. The thief, Cala, said Vossryn pays more than winter wool.

The repaired bell rang once at sunset, audible this time. Every shadow in Moonstairs snapped back to its owner, including Harlon’s, but his shadow pointed uphill. The missing survey crew did not return. Session ended with the village safer but the gate above glowing faintly.
